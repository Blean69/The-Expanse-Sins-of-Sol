THE EXPANSE — SINS OF SOL
INTEGRATED MILESTONE: SOL, THREE POWERS, SALVAGE AND SECRETS

Continue in the existing local repository. This brief replaces conflicting
earlier instructions, especially “keep only one playable faction.”
Preserve useful work already in progress; reconcile active workers
rather than starting duplicate implementations.

The target is THREE selectable faction identities sharing the SAME
current gameplay, plus a three-player Sol scenario, strategic salvage,
global announcements, Tycho infrastructure, and ProtoTech Composite
equipment.

This is staged implementation, not permission to rewrite the working mod.
All numbers below are initial design targets, not verified engine values.

- United Nations / UNN.
- Martian Congressional Republic / MCRN.
- Outer Planets Alliance / OPA.

Initially ALL THREE have identical:
- Available ships and heroes.
- Statistics, abilities, and ship voices.
- Prices and construction times.
- Fleet supply and economic modifiers.
- Research, structures, items, and technology access.

Only presentation and the Sol scenario's starting location differ.

No Earth population advantage, OPA extraction bonus, Martian quality
bonus, or faction-exclusive ships yet.

Share common definitions where supported. If the engine requires
duplicated wrappers, generate them from one maintained source.

Keep per-faction availability records for future restrictions, but
populate them identically now. Do not independently duplicate and
diverge the entire fleet three times.

Preserve proper per-owner research and limits. One player's research
must not upgrade opponents because definitions are shared.

Hero limits remain per player, not one shared global allocation among
the three clones. Audit filters, AI references, and default bonuses
inherited from the original TEC implementation.

Use differentiated names, descriptions, icons, and lobby identification.
Do not require another voice-generation project. Distinguish faction
identity from the freely chosen player color.

3. SOL: THREE POWERS — AUTHORED THREE-PLAYER SCENARIO

Create a reproducible fixed-layout scenario with one star, Sol, and
three human-playable starting empires.

This is an alternate-history sandbox, not exact canonical territorial
ownership or a scientifically scaled simulation.

Desired home mapping:

UNN -> Earth.
MCRN -> Mars.
OPA -> Jupiter — Jovian Habitats.

Jupiter ownership represents the Jovian orbital economy and habitats,
not a conventional surface settlement.

Prefer a functional home-node representation with Jupiter visuals.
If the installed homeworld mechanism cannot support that directly,
use a clearly labeled Jovian orbital-habitat home linked to Jupiter.

Do not silently relocate OPA to Ceres, give it an uncolonizable capital,
or create an unreachable invisible victory target.

FIRST prove the smallest three-home scenario, construction, ownership,
and victory behavior. Then expand the map.

Investigate automatic faction-to-home binding separately from fixed
slot ownership.

Prefer actual faction-aware assignment. If unavailable, implement a
deterministic documented setup:

Slot 1: UNN / Earth.
Slot 2: MCRN / Mars.
Slot 3: OPA / Jovian Habitats.

Use supported constraints or a clear lobby warning. Do not claim
arbitrary lobby ordering works until tested. Test host changes and the
slot/faction permutations relevant to the chosen implementation.

No random starts in the intended setup.

All players begin with one owned home, equivalent starting resources,
equivalent fleet/structures, viable build slots, and equivalent usable
economic capacity.

Nearby moons and colonies start unowned or under balanced militia.
OPA does not receive the whole Jovian system automatically.

Normalize scenario-specific home economics independently of visual size.
Do not globally reskin all vanilla planets to implement Earth or Mars.

Aim for approximately 24–28 playable/utility gravity wells:

Inner system:
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Luna.

Martian neighborhood:

Inner-system/Belt routes:

Jovian neighborhood:
Jupiter/Jovian Habitats, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto.

Outer destinations:
Saturn, Titan, Enceladus, Uranus, Neptune, Triton.

Add only a few relay/salvage nodes where needed for connectivity.
Pluto/Charon and further bodies belong in a later expanded map.
Do not fill empty space with dozens of resource-rich colonies.

Use compressed distances and readable body sizes. Place main planets
in recognizable radial order and moons near their parents.

Do not use literal astronomical distances, periods, planet radii,
or gravity-well proportions. Parentage, position, and travel links
are separate concepts.

Use documented parent/moon arrangements where compatible with the
installed build.

The first version should have a static/fixed orbital layout. Use
supported configuration and validate an extended session. An orbiting
variant can follow later.

No navigation or flip-and-burn rewrite.

CONNECTIVITY AND FAIRNESS

Create multiple corridors between regions.

Mars must not be the unavoidable bridge between Earth and OPA.
Do not create one Sun-centered shortcut connecting every home.

Aim for:
- Two useful outbound routes per home.
- Two comparable early expansion opportunities.
- Comparable travel time to contested Belt objectives.

Earth must reach the Belt without crossing Mars's home.
OPA must affect the inner contest without first conquering Mars.

Compare travel times using ordinary ships, not the Sunflare's extreme
burn ability.

Ceres, Eros, Luna, Deimos, and advertised colonies must actually be
contestable/colonizable through the intended progression.

Do not make Ceres itself an invulnerable minor-faction home. Put the
Ceres Shipping Exchange at a separately named Freeport/annex if NPC
rules require it.

Keep NPC homes off mandatory invasion chokepoints and preserve markets.

Balance first-expansion militia, colonization requirements, resources,
and build/research access. No player should need unavailable technology
simply to establish a viable opening economy.

Major salvage belongs on contested routes, not as free prizes inside
one home cluster.

5. SOLAR SYSTEM AND TYCHO ART

Use the existing Blender/glTF/MeshBuilder/material pipeline.
Keep original archives, licenses, editable derivatives, and generated
assets separate.

Prefer official NASA planetary assets/maps where their individual terms
permit use. Check package-specific credits and third-party restrictions.

NASA Ceres is the natural body, not a finished fictional settlement.
Add original settlement/dock details sparingly.

Verify UV projection, poles, seams, normals, color space, compression,
and lighting. Do not use a perspective planet photo as a seamless
spherical texture.

Prioritize Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Ceres, and Luna. Other bodies may use
appropriate local placeholders until ready.

Future settlement lights are artistic additions, not scientific data.
Avoid an unnecessarily huge texture package.

Audit the ewr2san Tycho STL candidate before rejecting its green preview.
Inspect its actual geometry and exact license.

For geometry-only STL, create suitable UVs/materials. Do not promise
to recover nonexistent original textures.

Proposed Tycho appearance:
- Grey industrial structural surfaces.
- Dark machinery and restrained contrasting panels.
- Clear docking markings.
- Warm habitat windows.
- Cool docking/work lights.

Use emissive textures for most small lights, not hundreds of dynamic
lights. Verify the shader's actual channel mapping.

Keep team-color and emissive masks independent. Avoid night-side
black-out and excessive bloom.

Separate habitat ring, hub, and weapon assemblies where useful.
Optional slow ring rotation can use the documented child-mesh system
after static export works.

Keep functional turrets on stable geometry unless rotating transforms
are verified. Client-render animation must not drive authoritative
gameplay.

No asset purchase without approval.
Art completion must not block functional map/contact/station tests.
Use an existing referenced starbase as a placeholder when needed.

7. TYCHO AS A MILITARY POWERHOUSE

Start from the proven starbase framework.

The station is expensive, slow-built, stationary, and shieldless without
explicitly fitted experimental equipment.

It should dominate an unsupported raid but remain vulnerable to a
committed siege. It must not replace the player's field fleet.

Initial weapon direction:
- Roughly 12–16 rotating dual-purpose PDC mounts.
- A modest set of torpedo banks.
- An optional Heavy Ordnance module adding two heavy railgun mounts.

Reuse working weapon families but tune TOTAL output. Do not multiply a
ship's entire intended weapon budget by every new mount.

Keep heavy-weapon tracking limits and saturation counterplay.
Exact mount count follows the asset and performance audit.

Offer limited upgrades, preferably choose three from four through
supported slot/access rules:

HEAVY ORDNANCE
Adds heavy railgun/torpedo capability.

REPAIR ANCHORAGE
Bounded local repair for owned ships.
Support allies only through an explicit supported alliance filter.
Cap throughput or simultaneous targets.
Avoid unlimited percentage-based healing of large capitals.

DEFENSE COORDINATION CENTER
Approximately +10% nearby PDC tracking.
Not a bonus to every weapon statistic.
Use a documented local radius and intended weapon filters.

INDUSTRIAL DRYDOCK
Approximately +15% local shipbuilding RATE where supported.
Do not describe +15% rate as exactly 15% shorter build time.

Each module has one clear primary role.

Test repair/coordination interactions with existing Scirocco support.
Same-effect auras must not multiply across overlapping stations.
Prefer the strongest applicable effect.

Effects end on station loss or leaving range.
No accidental effects in neighboring gravity wells.

Optional Emergency Lockdown:
Around 12 seconds of 20% reduced incoming damage to the STATION ONLY,
with a substantial cooldown. Add only after passive modules work.

No invulnerability, instant full healing, system-wide combat bonuses,
or unverified enemy-PDC shutdown.

Do not buff enemies, neutral visitors, mines, projectiles, or unrelated
structures. Test sieges with and without supporting defenders,
multi-direction attacks, and long-range pressure.

Limited component/reward; global operation announcement.

Research authorization, not free plating or finished composite.

Scouts discover sites. Major recovery requires an eligible capital or

Scirocco gets a Recovery Operations specialization.
Rocinante's Legitimate Salvage gives a modest advantage without erasing

Preserve existing combat abilities.

Start major recovery around 90 seconds, then tune against actual map
response times.

One claimant per site. Desired behavior:
Remain in range; leaving, destruction, or ownership loss interrupts and
releases the claim.

Ordinary damage should not continually reset progress unless explicitly
accepted as a necessary compromise.

Use supported persistent operation state. Resolve payment/reward once.

Handle simultaneous claims, cancellation, capture, full slots,
defeat/disconnect behavior, and save/reload.

Do not make reward delivery independent of the real objective state.

Do not create persistent wreckage from every ship death yet.
Prevent salvage farming from summons, scuttling, or ownership cycles.

Provide a costly late contact route or additional contested sample sites
so the first claimant cannot permanently lock everyone else out.

9. GLOBAL STRATEGIC ANNOUNCEMENTS

All active players, including enemies without local vision, hear about:

- Important recovery actually beginning.
- Its completion or genuine failure/abandonment.
- Paid protomolecule study actually beginning at its designated lab.
- Study completion or interruption.

Include player identity, event, and actual gravity-well/sector name.
Do not report every location merely as “Sol.”

Example templates, NOT assumed engine syntax:

“PRIORITY TRAFFIC: {player} has begun classified recovery near {sector}.”

“CONTAINMENT ADVISORY: {player} has begun protomolecule analysis
at {sector}.”

“RESEARCH INTERRUPTED: The containment facility at {sector} has
been lost.”

Start alerts occur after commitment, not selection, invalid orders,
or merely queueing.

Tie alerts and outcomes to the same authoritative operation.
Deduplicate and suppress restart spam without suppressing different sites.

Save/reload restores status but does not replay historical announcement
sounds or duplicate rewards.

Reveal the objective location, not surrounding fleets, unrelated labs,
research queues, or concealed units.

Use a supported marker/focus link where possible.
Never force camera movement.

Use one generic short sound plus parameterized text.
Routine salvage, ordinary research, composite fabrication, and individual
plating purchases remain owner-only.

Prove early warning FIRST with an enemy lacking local vision.
Owner-only or completion-only messages are not equivalent fallbacks.

12. IMPLEMENT THE ACTUAL PLATING ITEM

Adaptive Composite Plating is a mod-original per-ship item using a small
regenerating shield-backed layer. Explain that abstraction accurately.

Initially selected capitals and heroes only.
Identical eligibility for all factions.
One copy per ship, occupying a normal equipment slot.

Initial targets:
- Capacity about 12–15% of unmodified hull, set/capped by hull class.
- Regeneration after about 20 seconds without relevant incoming damage.
- About 1% of PLATING CAPACITY regenerated per second, not 1% of hull.
- No burst restoration or emergency full refill.
- No re-equipping refill exploit.
- Composite and ordinary resources consumed per installation.

Audit inherited TEC shield research, levels, items, auras, repairs,
restore abilities, bypass, drain, and shield-specific damage.

Renaming the buffer does not change its engine semantics.
Ships without the item remain shieldless.

Check whether bypass damage resets the intended recharge delay and
describe supported differences honestly.

Test installation/removal, upgrading, full slots, capture, refunds,
cancellation, and save/reload.

Separate ownership of research knowledge from ownership of a captured,
already-equipped ship.

Use a restrained hull-close effect where practical.
No mandatory shader rewrite or globally misleading interface rename.
